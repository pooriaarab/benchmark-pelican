# Changelog

## 0.2.0 - 2026-10-16

Scoring changes in this release make 0.1.0 results not comparable with 0.2.0 results.

- The grader now scores each task against its own `metadata.requiredParts`, and the score is multiplicative: a shared-structure gate times a task-specific fit that drops by 0.4 per missed or unrequested part. One memorised drawing scores at most 0.4 on a task it was not drawn for, and every generated reference scores exactly 1.0 on its own task. Abstract shapes cap at 0.2 through the bicycle-or-bird evidence gate.
- The scanner accepts honest formatting again: one surrounding code fence, prose around the block, an XML prolog, comments, `title`/`desc`/`text`/`tspan` elements, and the five XML entities all pass. `script`, `foreignObject`, `image`, event handlers, and external references still score 0.
- Warm-colour detection rejects cream and near-white fills. Legs must reach the pedal zone and handlebars must sit above the front wheel. Relative path commands resolve to real bounds.
- Task data now draws distinct seeded geometry per animal and vehicle pair. The hidden split uses disjoint animal and vehicle pools, a `hidden-` id prefix, and writes to `hidden/` outside `data/`, so it never ships in the npm package; it always requires a private seed (`--seed N` or `BPEL_HIDDEN_SEED`).
- The CLI, SDK, and API accept a task file override: `--tasks <path>`, `run(adapter, { tasksPath })`, and `createApiHandler({ tasksPath })`.
- Run reports expose `checkSummary` (pass rate per check) and per-row `details`, and the E2E report records measured adversarial scores. The MCP `run_benchmark` tool returns the same enriched report as the CLI, and `score(taskId, submission, { tasksPath })` grades against a generated split file.
- The drawing extent is the connected bird-plus-vehicle figure only: background rectangles, long thin ground or horizon lines, and detached decoration (a sun, a cloud) no longer stretch the extent or flip the wheel checks. A shape joins the figure only by touching what is already connected and only within one wheel diameter of the wheels or the bird's body, so a sun placed beside the bird's head cannot poison the score. Warm shapes attached to nothing but the head are the only beaks: a warm sun near the head can neither fake a beak nor reclassify one. Every score row also reports the measured extent under `details.extent`.
- Checks that could not be derived from the prompt text were deleted or restated: the long/standard wheelbase split is gone, wheel size is measured against the wheelbase, a battery pack must be nearly as wide as a wheel so a rectangular saddle cannot read as one, a second saddle is any small flat seat shape between the wheels (ellipse or flat rect), pedals accept any small shape between the wheels, handlebars are measured against the wheel band instead of the whole drawing (a band the prompts now state, and never warm-coloured), "long legs" means thin legs dangling at least half a wheel radius below the wheel axles — the prompt states the number — so legs that merely reach the pedals near axle height never read as unrequested long legs, and each beak, bar, and tire threshold is stated in the prompt with its number. A tail fan must be taller than two fifths of a wheel radius, and a small shape within a wheel radius of the frame's top tube is seat hardware, so a saddle under a narrow upright body no longer reads as tail feathers. A deliberately naive policy that draws only what the prompt says — no shared code with the task generator, jittering what the prompts leave open (leg bottoms anywhere in [-0.2R, +0.3R] of the axle line, plus body and handlebar proportions) — earns a measured mean of 1.000 and at least 0.8 on all 30 tasks on the public split and on held-out test seeds 7, 99 and 4242, at 1000x600 and 640x480, and every generated reference scores exactly 1.0 on its own task across 30 held-out seeds.
- The last round went further: the grader no longer uses any whole-drawing extent. The wheels are the two similar, level circles a frame line or path actually reaches at both centres — a loose circle (a sun, a ball) is never a wheel, and a concentric outer ring hugging a wheel is a tyre ring, not a wheel. Every check is measured in wheel units (radius, wheelbase, axle line) or head widths, so a full-width ground line, a sky band, or a wider canvas cannot move any threshold: measured, every reference scores identically at canvas widths 640 through 1600 with a ground line, a ground band, or none. Knobby tires accept any stroke at or above the stated 0.28-wheel-radius minimum — a fat tire counts as a stronger rugged tire on a knobby task — while a dedicated fat-bike still separates by its swept handlebars. Small wheels mean a radius under a seventh of the wheelbase, as the prompt now states, and the generator spaces the wheel classes far enough apart that the split survives its own jitter. A second saddle is any small flat filled seat — ellipse, rect, or filled path — above the frame and no wider than a wheel; only a flat rect at least as wide as a wheel right over the rear wheel is a rack, and a battery pack must be nearly as wide as a wheel and at least a third as tall as it is wide. A pouch is a second warm shape hanging clearly below the head's centre line and starting under the head, so a warm dot inside the beak's own bounds can no longer reclassify the beak. A seeded sweep of 200 suns scattered over the references moved 12 scores — every one a sun touching the figure; detached decoration never did. One limit stands: a loose circle the size of a wheel, kept at least 25 px clear of the figure, changed 117 of 3,600 scores (3.3%) in the reviewer's sweep, and a large circle above the axle line can read as the bird body.
- The seat-hardware bound now ends at the stated tail threshold: a shape up to 0.4 wheel radii tall near the frame's top tube is seat hardware, so a tail fan anywhere above the stated 0.4R — measured at 0.42R, 0.45R, 0.48R, 0.52R and 0.7R — scores exactly 1.0 on all 25 tail tasks across the public split and held-out seeds 7, 99 and 4242. A beak may be a filled warm shape or a stroked warm curve at least 0.06 wheel radii wide, and the prompts now say to draw the beak filled; a drop curl must be one curved stroke taller than it is wide, and the drop hint says so. The cited prompt-only numbers now come from an independent literal-number policy that draws strictly the stated numbers — measured mean 1.000, 30 of 30 at 0.8 or above on the public split and on held-out seeds 7, 99 and 4242 — while the builder's earlier tuned policy is kept only as a smoke test.
